## TL;DR

The widespread belief in meritocracy is rooted in simple, intuitive causal explanations and reinforced by psychological and cognitive factors. This belief persists despite evidence of structural inequalities, influencing attitudes toward equity policies and resource allocation.

## Why it matters

Understanding why meritocracy beliefs are so pervasive helps address social inequalities and informs strategies for promoting equity-oriented policies. Debunking the myth is essential for building fairer societies and increasing support for interventions that reduce inequality.

## Key technical takeaways

- Meritocracy beliefs stem from motivated reasoning and cognitive ease, making them psychologically comforting and easily accessible.
- People underestimate the impact of structural factors and overestimate individual effort in success.
- The dose-response schema (more effort = more success) is a familiar, intuitive explanation that shapes judgments from an early age.
- These beliefs legitimize existing hierarchies and reduce support for redistributive policies.
